## 2. Segments and sizes

- Jains in India: 4,451,753 (0.37%) in Census 2011, about 80% urban; Maharashtra 1.40M, Rajasthan 0.62M, Gujarat 0.58M. https://census2011.co.in/data/religion/6-jainism.html and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jainism_in_India
- Jain dietary strictness: 92% of Jains say they are vegetarian; 67% abstain from root vegetables such as onion and garlic (sample 109 Jains, small). https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2021/08/17/6-facts-about-jains-in-india/
- Implication: about 3M Indian Jains avoid root vegetables (estimate: 67% x 4.45M). The Census figure is 2011 and likely understated today.
- Jain diaspora: not found from a reliable source. An unsourced wiki page claims 7M+ worldwide; do not use.
- Vegetarians generally: Pew 2021 says about four in ten Indians call themselves vegetarian. https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2021/07/08/eight-in-ten-indians-limit-meat-in-their-diets-and-four-in-ten-consider-themselves-vegetarian/ . NFHS-5: 23% of women and 15% of men never eat meat/fish/chicken. https://theprint.in/india/vegetarianism-rules-in-north-india-but-dal-and-paneer-as-proteins-punch-below-their-weight/916827
- Swaminarayan / satvik / no-onion-garlic eaters outside Jains: not found as a counted segment. Only Pew's Hindu figure is available (44% vegetarian), no onion-garlic split.
- Vegans: no national figure. An NFHS-4 proxy shows about 1.1% of mothers' households had no dairy, eggs, meat or fish. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7269725
- Allergy households: prevalence is low in studies, about 1.2% of adults (Karnataka), 0.8% of Delhi-area schoolchildren. https://www.indiaenvironmentportal.org.in/articles/prevalence-of-food-sensitization-and-probable-food-allergy-among-adults-in-india-the-europrevall-inco-study and https://doaj.org/article/34cbaf7b1b604d1b83c679262270b13a . Small segment, high stakes (liability risk).
- Label-conscious urban shoppers: no direct size found. Proxy only: TruthIn at 150k MAU, 1.3M downloads (reported).

## 3. Least-served wedge

- Health-score slot: crowded and politically hot (TruthIn, Yuka-style, FoodPharmer, FSSAI rating coming).
- Diet-compatibility slot: Jain/satvik apps exist but are free, tiny, and mostly ingredient-photo or French-data based (Veg Guarantee shows French ingredient text per reviewer). No evidence of an India-first barcode database with Indian ingredient names (kanda, lehsun, hing nuance, E-codes, gelatin/carmine) at verified quality.
- Defensible? Moderately. Defensibility comes from: (a) rule quality and community trust, (b) Indian-product data coverage and corrections, (c) never making health claims, which avoids the legal fight. It is not defensible on technology alone since a generic AI scanner can be pointed at Jain rules in a day. Expect a niche, not a mass market.
- Honest ceiling (estimate): 3M root-vegetable-avoiders, with perhaps 1 to 3% of them ever active users in year 1 gives 30k to 90k users. This is an estimate, not sourced.

## 5. Zero-budget GTM and 90-day plan

Channels (no traction numbers found for any; these are tactics, not forecasts): Jain community WhatsApp groups, sangh and temple noticeboards, Paryushan and Navratri/Ekadashi season content, Instagram reels ("is this snack Jain?"), Reddit r/india and r/jainism, Open Food Facts contribution parties (builds data and goodwill), Jain student groups and college societies, local Jain caterers and tiffin services, diaspora groups via JAINA-type networks.

- Days 1-30: Fix top 200 Jain-relevant Indian products (biscuits, chips, namkeen, instant noodles, sauces). Publish a public ruleset (what is flagged and why). Recruit 20 Jain beta testers from family networks. Add an in-app "report wrong result" button. Post 12 reels.
- Days 31-60: Share in 30 WhatsApp groups through trusted community members, not spam. Contribute corrections back to Open Food Facts. Approach 3 Jain bloggers or Instagram creators. Add Amazon affiliate links on product pages.
- Days 61-90: Run a Paryushan-style "label check" campaign. Test a Rs 99 to 199/year supporter tier. Pitch 5 Jain-friendly brands or caterers for a clearly labelled, non-ranking verified listing. Targets (estimate, not sourced): 2,000 weekly users, 30 paying.

## 6. Legal and reputation risks

- Defamation: Mondelez sued FoodPharmer over 150+ posts about Bournvita and Tang; the court found a prima facie case, then clarified he may make factual comments. https://lawchakra.in/high-court/bournvita-foodpharmer-facts-belittle-delhi-hc . Madras HC granted a permanent injunction in the Triguni/EzeEats suit (Rs 10 lakh damages sought). https://www.storyboard18.com/how-it-works/madras-hc-grants-permanent-injunction-against-food-pharmer-in-ezeeats-triguni-suit-57683.htm
- Lesson: rating brands with opinionated labels ("unhealthy") invites suits. Keep outputs factual: show source data, show the rule triggered ("contains onion powder"), cite OFF, and avoid adjectives about brands. This fits the no-health-claims rule.
- FSSAI Indian Nutrition Rating: draft proposed September 2022 (0.5 to 5 stars). Supreme Court told the Centre in April 2025 to finalise front-of-pack norms within three months; in February 2026 it was dissatisfied and urged warning labels; as of the latest report found (August 2026, secondary source) not finalised. https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/advertising-marketing-branding/1612724/supreme-court-gives-centre-three-months-to-finalize-nutrition-labels-on-packaged-foods , https://www.livelaw.in/top-stories/supreme-court-unsatisfied-with-fssai-asks-it-to-consider-front-of-pack-labels-warning-of-high-sugar-fat-sodium-in-food-523200
- Implications: label the local Nutri-Score and composition score as informational, non-official; if INR or warning labels are notified, the app's scores may confuse users, so be ready to swap in the official rating. Also, a Jain check is a religious-dietary aid, so disclaim data errors; wrong "Jain OK" on allergen-like items is a reputational risk (not a legal finding).
- No FSSAI action against any app was found in my searches. Specific FSSAI rules on third-party rating apps: not found.

## Recommendation

Wedge: Jain-first dietary compatibility checker for Indian packaged foods (onion, garlic, root vegetables, gelatin, egg, honey), then satvik, vegan, and allergens. Monetization path: free core, then Amazon affiliate and a Rs 99 to 199 per year supporter tier (test), then verified-listing sponsorship from Jain-friendly brands once there are a few thousand active users. Keep the health score secondary and heavily disclaimed.
