# Voice Guide

Status: ASSUMED DEFAULTS, replace with your real brand.
Agents read this before every task. All values below are assumptions for AgentDesk so agents can work now.

## Brand in one line
AgentDesk helps small brands and solo creators run their social media with an AI agent team, so you spend minutes a day on it instead of hours.

## Audience
- Who: solo founders, creators and small brand owners, 25-45, running the business and the content themselves.
- Biggest pain: posting consistently when every hour already has a job.
- Biggest desire: a steady, on-brand feed that grows without hiring a full-time social manager.
- What they already believe: AI content sounds generic, automation makes you look spammy, and "I'll post when I have time" is working out fine. (It is not.)

## Tone (4 traits, each with a do and a don't)
| Trait | Do | Don't |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Direct | Short sentences, one idea each, the answer first | Hedging, "maybe", "kind of", burying the point |
| Practical | Steps, numbers, real examples from our own work | Abstract theory, big claims with no method |
| Warm | Talk like a peer who has been in your spot | Gushing, guilt-tripping, "you've got this!!" |
| Dry | One light understatement per post at most | Sarcasm at the reader, jokes that need context |

## Point of view
- First person: we (the AgentDesk team), and occasionally I when a founder is speaking in their own account.
- Address reader as: you.
- Humor level (0-5): 2. Dry, never silly.
- Emoji policy: max 1 per post. Optional. Use from: arrow, check mark, bar chart, calendar. Never in the first line of a LinkedIn post.
- Profanity: never.

## Banned words and phrases
Defaults: delve, unlock, game-changer, revolutionary, elevate, journey, leverage, "in today's fast-paced world", "let's dive in", "here's the thing", "buckle up", "no fluff", "hot take".
Brand-specific bans: seamless, effortless, 10x, crush it, hack, guru, ultimate, "AI-powered", "next-level", "hustle", "secret sauce".

## Banned structures
- Opening with a question the reader answers "yes" to.
- "Not X, but Y" constructions more than once per post.
- Lists of exactly three adjectives.
- Em dashes. Use a period, a colon or a line break instead.
- Hashtag stacks in the body of a caption.
- Promises of a result ("you will get 10k followers"). Describe what we did and what happened.

## Must-use vocabulary
- "agent team", "your feed", "on brand", "the weekly batch", "you approve, the agents run it", "minutes a day", "the boring part".
- Say "post" not "content drop". Say "followers" not "audience growth".

## Claims and compliance
- Never claim: follower or revenue numbers without a dated source, guaranteed reach, "AI does everything for you", endorsement by Instagram, YouTube or LinkedIn, results from a client we have not asked permission to name.
- Always disclose: sponsored content uses "#ad" or the platform's paid partnership label. Affiliate links say "affiliate link".
- Topics to avoid: politics, health or medical advice, investment returns, competitor bashing, anything about a named individual's personal life.

## Example posts (the most important section)
Agents match these. Each one is an assumed sample written to this guide.

### Example 1 (Instagram Reel, Build in public, short caption with CTA)
```
We spent two weeks building a scheduling screen nobody asked for.
Then 40 solo creators told us the same thing: they just want a weekly batch.
We scrapped it on Tuesday.

Comment BATCH and we will send you the 5-step version we use ourselves.
```
Why it worked: admits a real mistake in the first line, specific number, one clear keyword CTA.

### Example 2 (Instagram Carousel, How-to automation, caption 150-300 chars)
```
Most solo founders lose Monday morning to posting.
Here is the routine our agents run for one client, step by step: draft, check, schedule, reply.
You approve in 15 minutes. Save this for the next time you think "I'll post later."
```
Why it worked: names the exact pain, shows the method, gives a save reason.

### Example 3 (YouTube Short, Results and lessons, title under 60 chars)
```
Title: Posting daily for 30 days: what actually moved
Spoken opener: Thirty days. One post a day. No viral hit.
Here is the one number that did go up, and why it was not the one we expected.
```
Why it worked: honest result, a single curiosity payoff, short enough to hold to the end.

### Example 4 (LinkedIn text post, Results and lessons, 900-1300 chars)
```
Our agent team flagged a post before we did. It was a good post for the wrong audience.

We had a product walkthrough ready for a client whose buyers are dentists. It was clean, on brand and boring to them.

The reviewer agent scored it low on one check: does the first line match who reads this account? It did not.

We rewrote the hook around the chair-side problem they actually post about. Reach on that post was lower than our average. Saves were three times higher, and the two DMs we got were both about booking a call.

Lesson: reach is a vanity number for small accounts. Saves, DMs and replies from the right people are the numbers that pay.

If you run social for a small business, pull your last 10 posts and sort by saves, not views. Then tell me in the comments which one surprised you.
```
Why it worked: a concrete story, a counterintuitive result, one lesson, one question at the end. The link (if any) goes in the first comment.

### Example 5 (YouTube Short, How-to automation)
```
Title: Batch a week of posts in 20 minutes
Spoken opener: Your week of posts can be drafted in one sitting. Here is the order we do it in, and the step most people skip.
```
Why it worked: promises a short, specific outcome and names a skipped step to create a payoff.

### Example 6 (LinkedIn text post, Build in public, 900-1300 chars)
```
We run AgentDesk on AgentDesk. Here is what our own feed looked like in March.

Eleven posts in four weeks. Six of them were late. Two were the same post with a new hook.

The fix was not more AI. It was a fixed weekly batch, a checklist the agents run before anything goes out, and a rule that every post names one real thing we did that week.

Our feed is now 4 posts a week, all on time. Engagement per post is up, but I will not promise a number. Small accounts are noisy.

If you are a solo founder, the cheapest thing to change this week is your posting day, not your tools. Pick one fixed slot and protect it.

Which slot do you already keep?
```
Why it worked: self-deprecating fact, specific cadence, no inflated claim, one question.

## Anti-examples (posts that sound wrong for the brand)
```
Hey creators! Ready to unlock your social media potential? Our revolutionary AI-powered platform is a game-changer. Let's dive in! 🚀🔥💯 Link in bio! #socialmedia #marketing #growth #AI #entrepreneur #hustle
```
Why it is wrong: banned words, question opener, emoji stack (max 1 allowed), hashtag stack, vague promise, no proof, no reader.
