## Transcript

**Q1. Walk me through a typical study day.**

Neha: I wake up around 5:30 and feel great for the first hour. Ideally 6 to 8 is Polity reading, then mock tests. Realistically I'm on my phone by 10 checking the batch group. On a good day I get 9 hours of study, on a bad day 6. The breaks are where I lose it. A "five minute break" becomes 40.

**Q2. What have you tried to control your phone?**

Neha: Three things. Forest, the app with virtual trees. It was cute for two weeks, then I'd open it and never press start. Then the phone's built-in digital wellbeing limits, where I just tapped "ignore limit." The big one was last year: I put my phone in a steel box with a timer lock. It lasted four days. I bought a cheap screwdriver and had it open by Thursday.

*Follow-up: What happened on Thursday?*

Neha: I wanted to see my mock rank. I told myself I'd only check the result. Then I opened the coaching group, then Instagram. Ten minutes. The problem isn't that I can't stop, it's that I don't trust myself. Blocking doesn't stop me from wanting it.

**Q3. Which apps can you not block?**

Neha: WhatsApp, obviously. Our Delhi batch group posts the daily current affairs PDF and test schedule. If I block it I miss a test deadline. Mom and Papa's calls too. Google Drive for notes, Maps for the route to the centre, and one Telegram channel with the daily quiz.

*Follow-up: Would you accept blocking WhatsApp except one group?*

Neha: WhatsApp doesn't let you block one chat. If the app can't do that, I'd just turn the whole thing off and be annoyed.

**Q4. Where does your time actually go?**

Neha: YouTube, mostly. I call it "study YouTube," but it's a lecture followed by autoplay into vlogs. Instagram Reels is the real drain. Twenty minutes, sometimes an hour, and I don't even enjoy it afterwards. Snapchat too, because my cousin messages me there.

**Q5. How do you feel about a physical object?**

Neha: I like that I'd have to walk to it and touch it. The drawer failed because I could open a drawer in four seconds. But honestly, I'd hate it at first, the way you hate a dog that won't stop barking. My worry is it looks like a toy. Papa would ask "what is that thing on your desk?" If it's Rs 999 it should look like a proper object, not a cheap gadget.

**Q6. Where would you keep it?**

Neha: Not on the desk, because that's where I'd stare at it. Probably my pencil box, in the study bag I carry to the library. Then I realised I'd carry it everywhere, so I could lose it. If I lose it, I'm locked out of my own phone. That scares me more than anything.

*Follow-up: Two emergency unlocks a day would cover a lost puck?*

Neha: For a Tuesday, yes. If I lose it on a Sunday before a mock, no. Two unlocks don't help if I can't get in at all.

**Q7. Parents: who pays and would they be involved?**

Neha: Papa pays. He'd love this, but he'd want to see reports. Mom would want to know I'm not on my phone at 2am. Both of them call me around 1pm and 8pm, so calls must be allowed. I earn about Rs 3,000 a month tutoring kids in my lane, and I'm not spending that on a puck myself. If I explain it as a study expense, Papa would pay. (Unverified how common this framing is across aspirant families.)

**Q8. Exam season or after exams?**

Neha: Right now is the urgent part. Prelims are in May, mains after that, so the pressure runs from roughly October to May (**unverified** against the official calendar). After the exam I'd probably stop needing it. But if I drop it for a week, the streak dies and I'd quit, so the product needs a way to come back.

**Q9. What worries you about trust and privacy?**

Neha: Two things. First, accessibility permission. If an app asks for that, I say no. I've seen apps ask for everything. Second, who is the company? If it's a small startup and it shuts down, my phone might stay locked. That's the real fear: a dead app locking me out during exam season.

**Q10. How would you cheat it?**

Neha: Easily. Uninstall, force stop, turn off NFC, use my brother's old phone for WhatsApp, or change the date. Honestly the cheat I'd really use is the emergency unlock. I'd use both by 11am and tell myself each time it was an emergency.

**Q11. What would make you stop?**

Neha: If it blocked my test link during a live mock. If it crashed and I was locked out. If breaking a streak made me feel guilty every day. I'd break it once, feel awful, and delete it. Forest died in two weeks because the novelty ended. I'd expect the same here.

**Q12. What would you tell friends in the coaching group?**

Neha: "Try it, it's a puck." Then they'd ask the price. At Rs 999 I'd say buy it. At Rs 5,700 I'd say it's expensive and people compare it to Brick. Someone would say "bhai, just be disciplined," and someone else would say "just use Forest." Word of mouth depends on whether it works in the first week.

**Q13. Price reactions. I'll read four prices.**

- **Rs 999:** "That's a nice book set. I'd try it with my own money in a second."
- **Rs 1,999:** "Hmm. I'd need Papa to see it, and I'd need to be sure it works first. If it works, I'd pay."
- **Rs 3,499:** "That's a lot. A test series costs about that and I'd actually use it. I'd say no."
- **Rs 5,700:** "Are you serious? I'd laugh. Only if Papa paid, and he'd ask why a puck costs that."

**Q14. What would you pay extra for?**

Neha: A report I can show Papa: real hours versus hours I claimed. A schedule that follows my timetable, like Monday to Saturday 6 to 9am. Auto-allow for my study group chats if that's possible. A group focus session with a batch friend, both locked, so it feels social. I'd pay once for those, not monthly.

**Q15. What didn't I ask that I should have?**

Neha: Does it work offline? Library Wi-Fi is bad. Does the puck work through a phone case with a metal ring? And what happens during a power cut or when the battery dies?

**Q16. If I gave you one today, what would you do in the first hour?**

Neha: Block Instagram, YouTube and Snapchat. Then I'd get annoyed when WhatsApp got blocked. I'd test the emergency unlock to see how it feels. Then I'd put it in my pencil box and forget about it until Papa asked.

## Analysis

### Top 5 pains

1. **Breaks become the leak.** The five-minute break turns into 40 minutes. Neha's failure point is the moment of transition, not the study block itself.
2. **Blocking is all-or-nothing, but her day is not.** She needs WhatsApp (batch PDFs, test schedules) and parent calls. Blanket blocking breaks her workflow, so she disables the tool.
3. **Past tools are reversible in seconds.** Drawer, timer lock, "ignore limit," Forest: each was undone in minutes to days. Willpower-based friction is not enough.
4. **Fear of being locked out.** A lost puck or a dead app means she cannot reach her own phone before a mock. This is a bigger fear than distraction.
5. **Money is someone else's.** She has small earnings, Papa pays, and any purchase needs a justification she can present at home.

### Top 5 objections

1. **"What if I lose it or the company disappears?"** Lock-in risk. Needs a recovery path that works without the puck or the vendor.
2. **Privacy and permissions.** Accessibility permission is an automatic no. She wants clarity on what data leaves the phone.
3. **"I'd cheat it within a day."** Uninstall, force stop, second phone, date change, emergency unlock.
4. **Looks like a toy.** Desk presence matters, and parents will ask questions.
5. **No per-chat exceptions.** If WhatsApp cannot be partly allowed, the product is unusable for her.

### Price answer

**Recommendation:** Anchor on Rs 1,999 as the main price, test Rs 1,499 against it, and do not go above Rs 2,999 in the first launch. Do not price at Rs 5,700.

**Reasoning (from this persona only):**
- Rs 999 is an impulse buy she can fund herself. It is a good entry point, but it may signal "toy."
- Rs 1,999 is the highest price she would pay with a parent-approved "study expense" framing. This is where the product is credible enough to justify to Papa.
- Rs 3,499 loses her because a test series competes for the same budget and she values it more.
- Rs 5,700 triggers ridicule and a comparison to Brick. Neha's reaction matches the brief's warning that the price would need strong justification.

**Caveat:** This is one persona with synthetic answers. Validate with 15 to 20 real aspirants and separately with parents. Brick's Rs 5,700 price is taken from the brief and is **unverified**.

### Buying triggers

- A bad mock result that makes her want control now.
- A parent asking for proof of study or offering to fund a "study expense."
- A batch friend who already uses it and reports a real result (word of mouth needs a week-one win).
- A free trial or a one-week refund guarantee, which removes the fear of a wasted purchase.
- Visible reports she can show Papa, which turn the purchase into a justifiable expense.

### Churn risks

- **Streak shame.** One broken streak, then uninstall. Neha said this directly.
- **Novelty decay.** Forest lasted about two weeks. Expect a similar curve without a reason to return.
- **Blocking the wrong app.** Missing a WhatsApp test deadline is an immediate exit.
- **Lockout events.** A crash, a dead battery, or a lost puck during a mock.
- **Post-exam drop-off.** Usage likely ends after each exam cycle (**unverified**), so churn is seasonal.

### Five recommendations

1. **Build a "keep reachable" list with named contacts and time windows.** Allow parent calls, and let users pick specific apps for specific hours. Do not promise per-chat WhatsApp blocking unless it is technically confirmed.
2. **Replace streak punishment with forgiveness.** Allow one missed day without a reset, and show a weekly report of real focus time that can be shared with a parent.
3. **Design for the lost-puck case before launch.** Offer a cheap spare puck, a printed recovery code, and a rule that the phone can always be unlocked from the app after a cooling-off period. Keep the two emergency unlocks, but add a delay so they are not instant.
4. **Put a plain privacy page first.** State "no accessibility permission," "usage data stays on device," and "if we shut down, your phone unlocks." This directly answers her biggest trust fear.
5. **Market to the parent-and-student pair.** Position Nope as a study expense with a receipt and report. Test landing pages at Rs 1,499 and Rs 1,999, and offer a batch-friend referral pack, not a discount on the single puck.

- Parents fund the purchase and accept the "study expense" framing across income levels (**unverified**).
- Target users have Android phones with working NFC. Lower-cost Android models may not (**unverified**).
- Lost-puck frequency and how users want recovery to work.
- Willingness to pay falls between Rs 1,499 and Rs 2,499 for a one-time purchase, and drops sharply above Rs 3,499.
- Users accept limited whitelisting (no per-chat WhatsApp control) if calls and key apps still work.
- Self-reported study hours are inflated. Validate with real usage logs, not interview claims.
- Usage persists across a full exam cycle, not just the weeks before a test.
