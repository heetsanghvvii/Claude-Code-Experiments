# nope: 10 Real Customer Interviews in One Week

The five persona interviews in `research/` were **simulated**. This week replaces them with 10 real people. The goal is not to hear that nope is a good idea. The goal is to find out what real people already do about scrolling, what they have paid for before, and what would stop them paying Rs 1,999 for this. Labels: **[A]** assumption, **[U]** unverified.

---

## 1. Who to talk to (broad, per founder direction)

The audience is anyone in India who wants to scroll less [F]. Recruit a spread, not a segment, so you can see whether the pain and the buying differ by life stage.

| # | Profile | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | Students or exam aspirants (18 to 25) | Strongest pain in the simulated research; check if it is real |
| 3 | Working professionals (24 to 40), at least one WFH | Work focus angle; bigger budgets |
| 1 | Freelancer or creator who needs Instagram for work | The hardest case (Karan) |
| 1 | Homemaker or parent who scrolls themselves (not buying for a child) | Bedtime and evening scrolling angle |
| 1 | Someone 40+ | Tests "broad" honestly |
| 1 | Someone who has bought a gadget or app *as a gift* for someone's habits | Gift angle |
| 1 | Wildcard: someone who saw a nope ad or post and did **not** join the waitlist (from week 2) | Why the funnel leaks |

**Must:** uses an Android phone daily, and says yes to "Have you tried to cut down on any app in the last 3 months?" **Prefer:** at least 4 of 10 outside your city (video call), at least 3 women, a mix of English, Hinglish and Hindi speakers. **Avoid:** close friends, family, colleagues who report to you, and anyone who works in marketing or startups (they give advice, not behaviour).

## 2. Where to find them (this week)

| Source | Action | Expected yield [A] |
|---|---|---|
| Your Instagram and WhatsApp status | "Doing 10 short chats about phone habits for a small product I'm building. 25 minutes, video or call, Rs 100 thank-you by UPI. Not a sales call." with a screener link | 3 to 4 |
| LinkedIn post | Same message, professional tone | 2 to 3 |
| Ask 5 friends to forward to one person they know (not each other) | Second-degree contacts are less polite than friends | 2 to 3 |
| The new @nope Instagram, a story poll: "Do you scroll more than you want to? Yes / Very yes", then DM the "very yes" voters | Also seeds the account | 1 to 2 |
| From week 2: people who joined the waitlist and people who chatted but did not pay | Real buyers and real drop-offs | 2+ |

**Screener (Google Form, 6 questions):** age band; city; phone brand and model; "Does your phone have NFC? (Settings, search NFC)"; "Which apps do you wish you used less?"; "What have you tried to cut down in the last 3 months?" (open text). Pick people whose answers are specific.

**Thank-you:** Rs 100 by UPI after the call [A], or nothing if they decline. Tell them up front it is not tied to what they say.

## 4. How to avoid leading questions

- Ask about **the past**, not the future. "When did you last..." beats "Would you...".
- Never pitch before the problem section is done. You will hear politeness.
- Don't name the problem for them. Ask "What's your relationship with your phone like?" not "Do you scroll too much?". Never say "addiction".
- When they say something interesting, ask "Tell me more" or "When was the last time that happened?" and then be quiet.
- Compliments are not data. "That's cool" means nothing. Money, time and introductions are data.
- Don't defend the product. If they attack it, ask "What would have to be true for that to work for you?"
- Ask the price question **last** and as a real choice, not "how much would you pay?".
- Talk less than 30% of the time.

## 5. The script (25 minutes)

### Opening (2 min)

"Thanks for doing this. I'm building something small and I'm trying to learn how people really use their phones. There are no right answers, and you won't hurt my feelings. Okay if I record so I don't have to type?"

### Part A: Their life, not our product (10 min)

1. "Walk me through yesterday with your phone, from when you woke up."
2. "Which apps take more of your time than you'd like? How do you know?"
3. "When was the last time you opened one of them and lost more time than you meant to? What were you supposed to be doing?"
4. "What have you tried to cut down? Walk me through the last thing you tried." *(Listen for: timers, uninstalls, phone in another room, apps like Forest, giving the phone to someone.)*
5. "What happened with it? Why did you stop?"
6. "Have you ever **paid** for anything to help with this, an app, a gadget, a course? How much? Who paid?"
7. "Where is your phone right now? Where is it usually at night?"
8. "Which apps could you never have blocked, even for an hour?" *(WhatsApp, UPI, work, family calls.)*

### Part B: Show, then shut up (6 min)

Show the 15 second demo reel (R1 or R2) and the landing page on their phone or by screen share. Don't explain.

9. "What do you think this is?" *(Tests clarity.)*
10. "Who do you think it's for?" *(Tests the broad positioning.)*
11. "What's the first question that comes to your mind?"
12. "Where in your home would the puck live?" *(Tests the "another room" angle.)*
13. Show the permission disclosure screen. "What do you make of this?" *(Top objection. Note their exact words.)*
14. "What would worry you about using this?" Then: "What would make that worry go away?"

### Part C: Money and commitment (5 min)

15. "It's Rs 1,999 once, no subscription, with a 30 day refund. Thinking of your own budget, what does that compare to?" *(Let them anchor; don't suggest.)*
16. "If you got one, who would pay: you, or someone else?"
17. The real ask, said plainly: "The first 50 ship in about 4 weeks. Would you like to pre-order one now? It's fine to say no; that's useful too." *(If yes, send the link. A real pre-order is the strongest signal you'll get this week.)*
18. If no: "What would have to change for this to be a yes?"
19. "Is there one person you'd send this to? Would you send it now?" *(Introductions are commitment.)*

### Close (2 min)

20. "What should I have asked that I didn't?"
"Thank you. I'll send the Rs 100 now. Can I message you if I have one follow-up question?"

### Hinglish versions of the key questions

- "Kal subah se raat tak aapne phone kaise use kiya, thoda bataiye?"
- "Kaunse apps pe aap zyada time de dete ho jitna chahte nahi?"
- "Pichli baar kab hua ki ek minute ke liye khola aur bahut time chala gaya?"
- "Kam karne ke liye kya try kiya hai? Kya hua uska?"
- "Raat ko phone kahan rehta hai?"

## 7. What answers would change the plan

Count across 10 interviews. Thresholds are **[A]**.

| If you hear... | Then change... |
|---|---|
| 6+ cannot say what nope is after the reel | The lead creative. Re-shoot R1 and R2 with a clearer first 3 seconds before spending on ads. |
| 6+ say the puck would sit next to the phone, not in another room | The lead angle. Move A2 (deliberate tap) or A5 (bedtime) ahead of A1. |
| 5+ would not grant the permission even after reading the disclosure | Trust content becomes the first ad, not retargeting; the founder considers the Usage Access fallback in `docs/play-store/accessibility-disclosure.md` [D]. |
| 3+ have phones without NFC | The NFC check moves to the ad itself ("needs NFC"), and the batch 2 product question (a version without NFC?) goes on the founder's list. |
| 7+ compare Rs 1,999 to something they'd never pay for a phone accessory | Run E4 (price test) earlier and push the two-pack and gift framing harder. |
| Most "who pays" answers are "someone else" | The gift angle (A7) and the "ask someone to gift it" message become core, not optional. |
| 0 of 10 pre-order and fewer than 2 refer someone | Pause ads. The problem is the offer or the product, not the reach. Re-run 5 interviews after fixing the top objection. |
| 3+ pre-order | Proceed with Meta ads in week 2 as planned. |
| Students and aspirants show far stronger pain and buying than everyone else | Bring it to the founder as evidence; broad stays the decision [F], but E10 and a student-angle ad may earn a place. |
| Professionals keep asking for iPhone | Count it. Add an iPhone waitlist line on the site to size demand. |
| Several mention "my phone is in my hand at 1am" (Karan's point) | Bedtime angle needs a better answer than "walk to the puck"; show the puck outside the bedroom. |

## 8. Synthesis (Sunday, 90 minutes)

1. Read all 10 note rows. Highlight exact phrases people used for the problem. These become ad hooks (use them, with no attribution and no claims).
2. Tally section 7.
3. Rank the top 3 objections by count. Check that the site answers each above the fold or within one scroll.
4. Write a half-page note: what changed, what stayed, what to test next. Send the top phrases and objections to the creative head.
5. Mark which persona hypotheses in `research/` were supported, contradicted, or untested. Do not quote the personas as evidence again.
