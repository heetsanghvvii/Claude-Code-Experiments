# Customer discovery: Karan (synthetic persona 5), testing "Nope"

**Status:** Simulated interview with a synthetic persona. Nothing here is real customer data. Every factual claim is marked **unverified** unless it is a value the brief supplied. Prices are the test price points from the brief. The Brick price (Rs 5,700) is taken from the brief and is unverified.

---

## Part 1: Interview transcript

**Interviewer (I):** Thanks for making time. We're testing a small NFC puck and an Android app called Nope. You tap your phone on the puck to lock chosen apps, and tap again to unlock. Before I show you anything, tell me about the last blocker you tried.

**Karan (K):** The last one was some "Zen" thing with a green leaf. Downloaded it on a Tuesday, felt very virtuous for about four hours. Then I opened Instagram in Chrome. Instagram's website doesn't care about your app blocker. Took me maybe 40 seconds.

**I:** Walk me through how you beat the others, specifically.

**K:** Three patterns. One: uninstall it. Most have an "are you sure" screen with a 30-second wait. I wait 30 seconds. Two: accessibility service. The good blockers need accessibility permission to see which app is open, so I switch that off in settings. Some apps nag you about it. Nagging is not blocking. Three: the browser, or the lite app.

**I:** You said "pause" in your notes. What about that?

**K:** Pause is the emergency unlock with a nicer font. The Zen one had one. I pressed it. Unlimited, too. My friend said that's not a blocker, it's a door with a doormat.

**I:** Did any of them ever stick?

**K:** Once. I paid for a year of one because I was angry at myself. I cancelled after 11 days. I'd proven a point to nobody.

**I:** How much time are you losing now?

**K:** I don't count, and I won't pretend I have a number. The real loss isn't the scroll. I open Reels at 2am "to research hooks," and 90 minutes later I'm watching a dog in Ohio. The research never comes back.

**I:** I hear that, but you're avoiding the question. If you had to guess?

**K:** Don't make me guess. I'll lie. Say "more than my clients would like."

**I:** Fair. What do you actually need Instagram and YouTube for?

**K:** Instagram: posting my own reels from the phone, client DMs (half my work comes through there), and watching references. YouTube: client references, After Effects tutorials, and video essays I'm not supposed to be watching. Reels is the trap. Feed and Explore are the trap too, but Reels is the worst.

**I:** So if you could block Reels and keep DMs, would that work?

**K:** In theory. In practice a client sends me a reel link to edit. I tap it, it opens in the app, and now I'm in the feed. I'd have to not tap it, or the blocker would have to let me through once. Otherwise it's a fight every time a client messages me.

**I:** So link handling matters as much as the feature toggle?

**K:** Yes. Nobody builds for that. They build for the settings screen.

**I:** What about schedules? Something like "no Instagram 10pm to 7am."

**K:** Everyone builds schedules. They work until I have a 10pm client call. Then I turn the schedule off and I'm gone. A schedule with a manual override is just an emergency unlock wearing a tie.

**I:** Let's talk about the puck. Does tapping a physical object add real friction?

**K:** A bit. Walking to the puck is the friction. I'd put it on my desk, and at 1am I'm in bed with my phone in my hand. So I'd keep it in the kitchen. Then I'd think, "I'll just go grab the phone," and I'd still be on the phone. The tap itself takes two seconds. Honestly, it's a gimmick unless it makes me get up.

**I:** Many people say the physical act creates a moment of decision. Doesn't that matter?

**K:** It matters for people who don't have their phone in their hand at 1am. I always have my phone in my hand at 1am. Don't sell me the moment, sell me the fact that I have to leave the bed.

**I:** Walk me through how you'd beat this one.

**K:** Uninstall the app. Turn off its accessibility permission. Use the browser. Get my old phone out of the drawer. Or reset the phone, which is annoying but I've done it. Or I just leave the puck in a drawer and say I forgot.

**I:** If the app can't be uninstalled, what then?

**K:** Nothing can stop uninstall unless it has device admin rights. I'd find that annoying and then turn it off in five minutes. Don't build a fortress. Build something that makes me admit I'm doing it.

**I:** Two emergency unlocks a day, plus streaks. Reactions?

**K:** Two a day is two more than I need. Streaks are cute, my niece would love it. Duolingo has streaks and it's a guilt machine. If I break a streak because I used an emergency unlock, tell me you're not going to punish me for it.

**I:** So what would make you trust a product like this?

**K:** Tell me who made it, and tell me exactly what it can see. Does it send my app usage anywhere? If it's a solo founder in Bengaluru, say so. Don't say "AI-powered" and "science-backed" on the same page. And don't let it brick my phone if I uninstall it.

**I:** Let's talk price. Would you pay Rs 1,499, Rs 2,999, or Rs 5,700?

**K:** Rs 5,700 for a puck? I've seen Brick. People who buy that are buying a ritual. Rs 1,499, I'd try it once to see if I'm lying to myself. Rs 2,999 is what I'd call a real product with a case and a warranty. Rs 5,700 I'd laugh at, unless it's a permanent fix, and nothing is.

**I:** Why does Brick matter to you?

**K:** It doesn't. Someone will say "Brick is 5,700, so this is cheap." That's their problem. I compare it to what I already lose for free.

**I:** You paid for a blocker for a year, though. Doesn't that show you'd pay for a fix?

**K:** It shows I'd pay to feel better for a week. Be careful with that.

**I:** Fair. If you were going to try this, what would the refund policy need to look like?

**K:** Thirty days, no interrogation, no "tell us why." If I lose the puck, I need a way out that isn't a three-day support email. And a refund must not leave my phone locked. Also, no subscription. That's the only reason I'd try it at all.

**I:** What marketing would win you over, and what would you mock?

**K:** Win me: "It's a puck. It doesn't stop you. It makes you choose." Mock: "Reclaim your life," "Break your addiction forever," and "increases focus by 300%." Put a number on it and I'll ask where it came from.

**I:** Would you post about it?

**K:** If I tried to beat it on camera and it lost, I'd post that. Three seconds of me failing is content. If it won, maybe a quiet story. I don't do sponsored stuff unless the thing is actually good.

**I:** What would you need to see in the first week to keep it?

**K:** My DMs work. Reels are blocked. A client link doesn't turn into a fight. The streaks don't guilt me. That's it. Four things.

**I:** What did we miss?

**K:** You didn't ask about my editing laptop. Everyone asks about phones. And make the puck small. I'm not carrying a coaster around.

## Part 2: Analysis

### Top 5 pains

1. **Blockers fall in under a minute.** Uninstall, accessibility toggle, or the browser. Karan's trust in blockers is already gone.
2. **The loss is research time, not scroll time.** Reels is the trap, and a single link opens the feed.
3. **Blunt blocks break income.** Client DMs are where his work arrives, so a whole-app block is unusable.
4. **Schedules fail on real exceptions.** A late client call turns the schedule into a permanent override.
5. **Guilt mechanics and hype breed resentment.** Streaks and "science-backed" claims read as manipulative.

### Top 5 objections

1. "I'll uninstall it or turn off accessibility." (bypass)
2. "The tap is a gimmick. My phone is in my hand at 1am."
3. "Two emergency unlocks a day is basically unlimited."
4. "Rs 5,700 for a puck is absurd. Rs 2,999 needs a reason."
5. "Who made it, what can it see, and will it brick my phone?"

### Price answer

- **Recommendation:** Lead with Rs 1,499 as the entry price. Test Rs 2,999 only as a bundle with a case, warranty, and clear refund terms. Do not anchor against Rs 5,700 in marketing.
- **Reasoning from the transcript:** Karan would try Rs 1,499 once, would consider Rs 2,999 for a product that feels durable, and rejects Rs 5,700 outright. He values the product on time saved, not on the gadget. His stated willingness is a single synthetic data point, so it is a hypothesis, not a market price.
- **Validation:** Run a split test across the three price points with real users, and measure trial-to-keep at 30 days, not click-through.

### Buying triggers

- A concrete failure: a missed client deadline or a late-night research spiral.
- A refund policy with no interrogation.
- A promise that DMs and client links still work.
- A visible "I tried to beat it" demo that fails.
- Proof from a peer who is also a creator.

### Churn risks

- The puck is left in a drawer, or the user forgets it.
- Emergency unlock is so easy it becomes the default.
- The first client call is blocked and the user has no clean override.
- Streak breaks create guilt and the user uninstalls.
- Accessibility or permission state changes after an Android update, and the lock silently stops working (unverified, needs testing on current Android versions).

### Bypass methods, ranked by likelihood, with Android preventability

| Rank | Bypass | Preventable on Android? |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Use the browser or web version | Partly. A local VPN-service domain block can cover instagram.com, but users can switch DNS or turn off the VPN. Lite and other apps may need separate handling (unverified). |
| 2 | Uninstall the app | Partly. Device admin can block uninstall, but users can disable it. A delay or cooldown adds friction without a hard block. |
| 3 | Turn off accessibility or usage access | Partly. The app can detect the state and re-lock or nag, but cannot stop the user from changing settings. Restricted-settings rules vary by install source (unverified). |
| 4 | Emergency unlock abuse | Yes. A daily cap, a cooldown, and a visible log are fully within the app's control. |
| 5 | Change the device clock to end a schedule | Partly. Checking network time can detect tampering, but not every case (unverified). |
| 6 | Use the old phone or a second phone | No. Outside the app's reach. |
| 7 | Lose the puck, or leave it elsewhere | Partly. Only by design, for example a recovery path that does not require the puck. |
| 8 | Reset or reinstall the phone | No. Outside the app's reach. |

### 5 recommendations

1. **Make the promise honest.** "It makes bypassing a decision, not impossible." Remove "break your addiction" and any unsourced numbers.
2. **Build per-feature blocking with an exception list.** Allow DMs, block Reels and Explore, and offer a one-tap, logged exception for opened client links. Test this with working creators first.
3. **Replace streak guilt with a visible cost.** Keep two emergency unlocks, but log each one, show the count, and never punish streak breaks with loss framing.
4. **Publish a refund policy and a puck-lost path.** Thirty days, no reason required, no bricked phone, and a recovery route that does not depend on the puck. Publish exactly what the app can read.
5. **Price at Rs 1,499 and test a Rs 2,999 bundle.** Drop Brick comparisons from marketing. Use a "try to beat it" video as the launch asset, since it doubles as proof and content.

### Assumptions to validate

- Karan's bypass speed is representative. This is one synthetic persona, so it needs real interviews.
- A Reels-only block with a link exception is enough for creators who also do client work.
- People will keep the puck within reach at night.
- Current Android behavior for accessibility, device admin, and restricted settings matches what we expect (unverified).
- Refund and recovery costs are acceptable at Rs 1,499.
- Indian buyers respond to Rs price points the way the synthetic persona suggests (unverified).
- Customers want a hardware puck at all, versus a software-only version at a lower price.
