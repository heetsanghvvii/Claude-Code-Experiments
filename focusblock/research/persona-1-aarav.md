# Persona 1: Aarav. Simulated Customer Discovery Interview

**Status:** SYNTHETIC. This is a simulated interview for hypothesis generation, not real customer evidence. No statistics or studies are cited. Any India or market fact is marked **unverified**.

**Product tested:** "Nope", a small NFC puck plus an Android app that blocks chosen apps (Instagram, YouTube, etc.). Tap phone on puck to lock, tap again to unlock. Two emergency unlocks per day, streaks, daily focus time. No subscription. Android first.

**Price points shown:** Rs 999, Rs 1,999, Rs 3,499, Rs 5,700 (competitor Brick sells at Rs 5,700).

---

## Interview Transcript

**Interviewer:** Thanks for doing this, Aarav. Nothing to sell, no wrong answers. Walk me through yesterday on your phone, from waking up.

**Aarav:** Alarm goes off at 7 and I don't get up. I open Instagram before I even see the time. I tell myself I'm checking messages, but it's Reels. Then YouTube Shorts in the bathroom. In lectures I have notes open on one side and Reels on the other. Last night Digital Wellbeing said 5 hours 10 minutes and I was like, that's wrong. It was not wrong.

**Q2. Which apps take the most time, and how do you know?**

**Aarav:** Instagram, then YouTube, then WhatsApp, though group chats feel different. I look at Digital Wellbeing on Android. My cousin has an iPhone and his numbers look different, so I don't fully trust either.

- *Follow-up: Do you check those numbers regularly?*
- **Aarav:** Only when I feel guilty. Mostly at 2 am, and then I sleep worse.

**Q3. What have you already tried to cut down?**

**Aarav:** Loads. I set a 30-minute Instagram limit in Digital Wellbeing and tapped "ignore for today" every time. In October I uninstalled Instagram for nine days. I used the browser version and it was worse, because the browser didn't feel like it had any limits. I reinstalled because the fest coordination group is only on Instagram. Half my college life is on there for a reason.

**Q4. When do you open Reels without meaning to?**

**Aarav:** Three times, mainly. Right after waking up, in the ten-minute gaps between lectures, and after dinner when the common room goes quiet. Exam weeks are the worst. I avoid studying, then open Shorts to avoid the guilt about avoiding. It's a loop.

**Q5. Who influences what you buy or install?**

**Aarav:** Friends, a lot. Rohan bought a keyboard after one reel and now half the floor wants one. If my friends use something, I try it. My UPI limit is tight and I count every rupee. But I spent about Rs 1,400 on an in-game thing in one night because the group was doing it.

**Q6. Who would you buy this for?**

**Aarav:** Myself, probably. My mom might pay if I say it's for studies, because she checks my phone when I'm home. Rohan is worse than me but would never admit it, so he'd never buy one. Maybe I'd buy two and give one to him as a joke.

**Q7. I'll describe the product. It's a small round NFC puck. You choose apps to block, like Instagram and YouTube. You tap your phone on the puck to lock them and tap again to unlock. First reaction?**

**Aarav:** Cool idea. Second reaction: where do I keep the puck? If it's on my desk and I'm in the library, am I locked out of my phone all day? I'd need one on my desk and one in my bag, so that's two. Honestly the physical thing works on me. If it's on my bed frame, walking over to it is annoying enough that I'd probably stay off my phone.

- *Follow-up: Would you carry it?*
- **Aarav:** Maybe. I'd lose it within a week. I lose pens.

**Q8. The app has two emergency unlocks a day, streaks, and daily focus time. How do those land?**

**Aarav:** I'd use both emergency unlocks before lunch on day one. I'd say WhatsApp for the fest group counts as an emergency every day. I like streaks. I have a language-app streak at 300 days. I don't know if I'd keep one for a puck. Focus time is nice, but I'd probably just watch the timer.

- *Follow-up: Does the emergency unlock make you feel trapped?*
- **Aarav:** Trapped, yes, but in a good way at first. Then in exam week I'd think two unlocks isn't enough for the study playlist I need.

**Q9. Android needs accessibility permission for this. What goes through your head?**

**Aarav:** That's the scary part. Accessibility means it can see what's on screen, right? I read a thread where someone said an app was reading their messages. I don't know if that's true. If the app says "we don't read your content" in a tiny line, I won't trust it. My UPI app is on this phone, so if anything had access to that, I'd be done. I'd probably install it, check the permissions, and maybe uninstall.

- *Follow-up: What would make you feel safe?*
- **Aarav:** Plain words, not legal ones. "This app can see which app is open. It cannot see your messages." And a way to see that in Android settings. Also, I'd want to know the puck isn't a tracker.

**Q10. I'll show four price points: Rs 999, Rs 1,999, Rs 3,499, and Rs 5,700. A competitor called Brick is at Rs 5,700. Reactions?**

**Aarav:** Rs 999, I'd buy tonight. I'd think for an hour and then buy. Rs 1,999, I'd have to ask mom. Rs 3,499, I'd laugh. Rs 5,700, that's for people with money, and I'd be annoyed if it broke. Brick, I haven't heard of it. If it's Rs 5,700 for a puck, what makes it worth that? I'd check Amazon first. Also, I did spend Rs 1,400 on a game last month, so my number might be higher than I think.

**Q11. Where would you first hear about something like this?**

**Aarav:** Reels, but not an ad. A student creator saying "I did this for 30 days and my screen time went from six hours to two." I'd believe a student over a company. Maybe a productivity subreddit. I'd only search the Play Store if someone told me the name first.

**Q12. What would make you stop using it after a week?**

**Aarav:** The fest group. If I can't reach the group chat or event DMs, I'm done. Also a lost puck, or bugs where I have to reboot my phone. And boredom. Day three the novelty wears off. Day seven is when I decide if it's a habit or a toy.

**Q13. How would you cheat it?**

**Aarav:** Easy. Instagram on the laptop browser. The hostel computer lab. A friend's phone. Uninstalling is a loophole, not cheating. Honestly I'd probably use the emergency unlock every day. You'd have to block the web too, and that's hard.

**Q14. What would you tell a friend?**

**Aarav:** "Bro, try it, it's annoying in a good way." I'd show Rohan and say he needs it, and he'd say I need it more. If I used it for a week I might post a reel about it with a bad cut, and say it's not sponsored. Maybe.

**Q15. What feature would you pay extra for?**

**Aarav:** A study mode that blocks everything except my notes app and the college portal, with no emergency unlock. A hostel mode: if five people in one hostel use it, we get a group leaderboard. That would make me pay. Calendar sync so it turns on when my exam timetable starts. I don't care much about streaks.

**Interviewer:** Thank you, Aarav.

**Aarav:** Ask me again in exam week. I'll either love this or throw the puck out the window.

## Analysis

### Top 5 Pains

1. **Unplanned gap time.** Reels and Shorts fill wake-up, between-lecture, and after-dinner gaps. He describes these as involuntary, not chosen.
2. **Exam-avoidance loop.** Scrolling becomes a way to escape the guilt of not studying, which makes the problem worse under stress.
3. **Failed willpower tools.** Timers get dismissed. Uninstalling breaks his social coordination (the fest group lives on Instagram), so he reinstalls.
4. **Late-night guilt and poor sleep.** Checking screen time at 2 am adds stress without changing behavior.
5. **Tight money with social spending.** He counts rupees but still makes group-driven purchases, and bigger buys need a parent.

### Top 5 Objections

1. **Accessibility permission and privacy.** He fears it reads everything, including UPI. He wants plain-language scope, not legal text.
2. **Puck logistics.** Lose it, forget it, or need two. Physical friction works for him, but a single-puck lockout may feel punitive.
3. **Leakiness.** Browser, laptop, and friends' phones all bypass the app. He calls this a loophole and expects to use it.
4. **Price above Rs 999.** Rs 3,499 and Rs 5,700 read as "not for me." The Brick comparison does not explain value to him.
5. **Emergency unlock and social apps.** Two unlocks a day will be used up on day one, and WhatsApp group coordination counts as an emergency.

### Price Answer

**For Aarav's self-purchase, the yes zone is around Rs 999. Rs 1,999 needs a parent or gift framing. Rs 3,499 and Rs 5,700 are clear rejections.**

Reasoning:
- He reacts to price in tiers, and his stated ceiling for self-funded buys is Rs 999.
- He contradicts this: he spent about Rs 1,400 on a game and bought a Rs 2,000 keyboard after peer influence. So his real ceiling is probably closer to a social-purchase threshold, and it moves with the group.
- He can justify a higher price if a parent pays or if the product is framed as a study tool (Q6, Q15).
- Brick at Rs 5,700 makes him ask what the premium buys. Without a clear reason, the anchor pushes him toward "too expensive" rather than "premium."
- Market context (typical student budgets, hardware cost, competitor positioning): **unverified**. Do not treat any of this as settled.

Recommendation: test Rs 999 and Rs 1,499 as primary anchors, with a two-pack option (desk plus bag) at a modest premium. Do not anchor on Rs 5,700.

### Buying Triggers

- A student creator showing a visible, honest result after 7 to 30 days (Q11).
- Friends using it, especially a group or hostel mode with a shared leaderboard (Q5, Q15).
- Exam weeks, when the scrolling guilt peaks (Q4).
- A parent-funded framing ("it's for studies") (Q6).
- A gift or two-pack for a roommate (Q6, Q14).
- A study mode that blocks everything except notes and the college portal (Q15).

### Churn Risks

- **Day three novelty drop, day seven decision point** (Q12).
- **Group chat breakage.** If WhatsApp or Instagram DMs for college events get blocked with no workaround, he leaves.
- **Lost or forgotten puck.** Lockout with no clear recovery path.
- **Bugs that require a reboot.**
- **Emergency unlock abuse.** Daily use turns the feature into a bypass, and exam-week need exceeds the limit.
- **Browser and friend's-phone bypass.** If the product feels leaky, the value disappears.

### Five Product or Marketing Recommendations

1. **Solve the group-chat problem before launch.** Let users whitelist specific threads or contacts (for example, a college group) without unlocking the whole app. This addresses the most common stated reason to quit.
2. **Make the accessibility permission legible.** Show a short "what this can and cannot see" screen before install, with a link to the Android settings entry. Publish a plain-language data-handling statement. Consider a third-party review of the permission scope before relying on trust claims.
3. **Test pricing with real people at Rs 999 and Rs 1,499, plus a two-pack.** Drop Rs 5,700 as a comparison point. Frame the two-pack as "one for your desk, one for your bag," since he already imagines that setup.
4. **Build the paid tier around study and group features.** Study mode (notes and portal only, no emergency unlock), a hostel or friend-group leaderboard, and exam-timetable calendar sync are the features he names. Keep the core free of streak dependence.
5. **Market through student creators with honest 7-day results.** Use rough, unpolished, "I tried this for a week" content, not ads. Include a lost-puck replacement policy in the messaging, since losing it is an expected event for this user.

### Assumptions to Validate With Real People

- Students will pay for a physical puck rather than use free Digital Wellbeing or other app-timer tools (**unverified**).
- Accessibility permission does not stop installs among Android students, and a clearer explanation actually changes trust (validate with real installs).
- The puck is carried or kept near the user, not lost or left behind at a rate that breaks the product (validate with a 7-day diary study).
- Two-puck purchases (desk plus bag, or a gift) are common enough to shape pricing.
- Rs 999 is the right anchor for self-purchase, and Rs 1,999 works with a parent or gift framing (run a real price test, not a survey).
- Group-chat dependence (fest coordination, hostel groups) is widespread and not just this persona's situation.
- The browser and friend's-phone bypass is frequent enough to matter, and blocking the web is feasible on Android (**unverified**, needs a technical check).
- Brick's pricing, features, and positioning (**unverified**, not researched here).
- Student creators are a real discovery channel for this product, and creator-led trust outperforms paid ads for this audience (**unverified**).
- Android-first is acceptable for the target segment. The iPhone share of the target users is **unverified**.
- A single synthetic interview is not evidence. Every assumption above needs real interviews, a landing-page or pricing test, and a usage test before it is treated as a decision.
