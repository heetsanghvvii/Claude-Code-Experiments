# Feedback, Testimonials and Proof

Owner: CCO. Date: 9 Nov 2026. Status: DRAFT.

Labels: **[F]** fact, **[A]** assumption, **[U]** unverified.

Current evidence of product success: none. Zero customers, zero real usage data. Every customer line in the plan so far is from synthetic interviews [S]. This document is how we earn real evidence honestly.

---

## 1. Collecting honest reviews and testimonials with consent

1. **Ask after a real moment, not on a schedule.** Best times: after the day 7 check in if the number was 4 or more, and at day 28 if they chose KEEP. Never ask someone who just asked for help or a refund in the same message.
2. **Ask for the truth, not praise.** "What is one thing nope did well and one thing that annoyed you?" Collect the annoying part too. A review with a small complaint is more believable.
3. **Get explicit consent in writing (WhatsApp is fine) and keep the message.** Use this wording:
   > "May we quote what you just wrote, on our website and ads? Reply YES to use your first name and city, YES ANON to use no name, or NO. You can change your mind any time and we will remove it."
4. **Show them the exact quote and where it will appear** before publishing. They can edit or decline.
5. **Do not pay for, or give a discount in exchange for, positive words.** A thank-you after the fact that is not tied to the content is acceptable, but disclose it. Check with the CLO on advertising rules for endorsements in India [U].
6. **Store consent:** date, exact quote, exact wording they agreed to, channel, how to remove. One row in the sheet from CHECKINS.md.
7. **Ask for the public review separately** (Play Store, Google). Make it easy and make it optional. Never ask only the happy ones if we can help it; send the link to everyone who answered the day 28 message and kept it.
8. **Refunded customers get a say too.** Ask: "What would have made it work for you?" Publish what we learn (in aggregate) if they agree. It builds trust.

## 2. How to use real quotes

- Quote exactly. Fix only spelling, and mark any trimming with "...".
- Attribute honestly: first name and city, or "Customer, Pune" if they chose anon. Never invent an age or job.
- Show the date and how long they had used nope when they wrote it ("after 3 weeks").
- Use the mix: at least one lukewarm or critical quote for every three positive ones on any public page [A].
- Keep the screenshot or message as proof, filed with the consent record, in case someone asks.
- Use a quote only for what the person said. Do not stretch "I scrolled less this week" into "reduces screen time by half".
- Put a small note near any testimonials: "From our first [N] customers. Early users, small group." State N.

## 3. What we must never fake

- Never write a review or testimonial ourselves, or have a friend or family member who did not use it write one. Never post under a made-up name.
- Never present synthetic personas (research/ folder) as customers. They are [S].
- Never show fake numbers: "10,000 users", "stars", countdown timers, "only 3 left" if untrue, invented screen-time savings.
- Never use stock photos as customers.
- Never claim results we have not measured: no "reduces screen time by X percent", no "cures addiction", no health or mental health claims [F: the claims line is kept simple].
- Never edit a negative review out of our own collection. Never hide a refund count.
- Never use a customer screenshot with other people's names, messages, or number visible.
- Never claim Play Store approval, "official", or a partnership that does not exist.
- If we do not know, we say "we do not know yet".

## 4. Week 4 interview script (10 questions, 20 minutes)

Use with people who bought, whether they kept it or not. Record only with their permission. Do not defend the product. Do not pitch. If they say something harsh, say "thank you, tell me more".

Intro: "Thanks for doing this. There are no right answers. I am trying to find what is wrong with nope. Is it okay if I take notes? Can I record, and I will delete it after I write it up?"

1. **Walk me through yesterday evening on your phone, from dinner to sleep.** (Reality check before nope.)
2. **When did you last tap the puck? What was happening just before?**
3. **Where is the puck right now? Take me through the last time you moved it.**
4. **Tell me about the last time you wanted to open a blocked app while locked. What did you do?** (Listen for bypass: emergency unlock, backup code, other phone, laptop, uninstall.)
5. **Which apps do you still lose time to, that nope does not touch?** (Substitution.)
6. **Was there a moment you felt stuck or worried you could not get in? What happened?** (Lock out fear.)
7. **What did setup feel like? What would you tell a friend to expect?** (Friction.)
8. **If you could not use nope tomorrow, what would you miss, if anything?** (Real value test. "Nothing" is a valid answer.)
9. **Would you recommend it to a friend? Who, and what exactly would you say?** (Listen for specifics, not "yes".)
10. **If you had to change one thing, what is it? And was Rs 1,999 worth it, a bit more, or a bit less?** (Price check, asked last and only after real use.)

After the call: write down 3 verbatim quotes, one bypass story, one friction story, and tick which of the 12 signals below it touched. Within 24 hours.

## 5. Twelve signals that the product is working or failing

For each: a "working" reading and a "failing" reading. All thresholds are [A] starting guesses, to be changed once we have data.

| # | Signal | Working | Failing |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Setup completion within 48 hours of delivery | 80 percent or more finish | Under 60 percent |
| 2 | First successful lock within 24 hours of setup | 85 percent or more | Under 65 percent |
| 3 | Setup help requests per 10 customers | Under 3 and falling | Over 5, or the same step recurs |
| 4 | Puck location at day 3 | Most say another room | Most say same room or drawer |
| 5 | Days locked in week 1 (self report) | Median 4 or more | Median 2 or fewer |
| 6 | Emergency unlock or backup code used (self report) | A few times, then fewer | Used almost daily by many, or never needed because nobody locks |
| 7 | Phone kills the service ("it stopped working") | Under 5 percent of customers | Over 15 percent, or concentrated on one brand |
| 8 | Day 14 helpfulness score | Average 3.5 or more | Average below 2.5 |
| 9 | Week 4 retention (3 or more days locked in the last 7, non-repliers count as not retained) | 40 percent or more | Under 20 percent |
| 10 | Refund rate by day 30 | Under 10 percent [F: plan milestone] | Over 20 percent [F: stop line in plan] |
| 11 | Unprompted referrals or "got one for my brother" | At least 1 in 10 customers mentions or buys again | Zero |
| 12 | Tone of free text feedback | People describe a changed moment ("I stopped before bed") | Only vague praise, or "I forgot about it" |

Reading rule: if signals 1, 2 and 7 are bad, the problem is setup and reliability, which is fixable. If those are fine and 5, 8 and 9 are bad, the problem is the idea or the habit, which is the real risk. Say which one it is in the weekly note to the CEO.
