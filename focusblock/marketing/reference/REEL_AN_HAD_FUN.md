# Reference reel analysis: @an_had_fun_ on Brick (56 s)

Source: instagram.com/reel/DeRooJkInHY, retrieved 9 Nov 2026 by the founder. Material: automated transcript (unchecked), 14 frames at 4 s spacing, caption. Claims in the reel are the creator's, not verified facts. Frames are not proof of how the product works or resists bypass.

## What the reel shows
- Creator buys Brick for what she says is Rs 11,000 "because I'm too undisciplined about my screen time".
- Flow she describes: download the app, choose apps (Instagram, LinkedIn, Messages, Threads, WhatsApp visible), tap the phone on the block to start focus mode, tap again to unlock.
- Her hook idea: send the block somewhere else (give it to her sister or parent to take to work, hide it in the house). She cannot reach the blocked apps until she physically gets back to it. One time purchase, no subscription. Plans "no scroll Sundays".
- Caption: she posted this on trials and learned that "some Indian brands are making the same thing for much cheaper" and will check them out and report back.
- Ends with a question: "Would you spend Rs 11,000 on a product like this?"

## Format notes (what works, for our creative brief)
- 0 to 4 s: price shock hook in the first sentence (Rs 11,000) plus the problem in her own words.
- 4 to 16 s: product on camera in hand, then two quick cutaways (app store page, app selection screen) with a bold title card.
- 16 to 52 s: talking head, word by word captions, the one idea that matters (friction by distance, hide it, give it away).
- Last seconds: an open question for comments. Honest, curious tone, "I'm so curious if this works", no hard sell.

## What it means for nope (hypotheses, not decisions)
1. Demand signal and a price anchor: someone paid a high price for a physical blocker and publicly asks if it is worth it. Our Rs 1,999 sits far below. Her Rs 11,000 does not match the Rs 5,700 we saw on Brick's page for one unit (could be several units, accessories, duty or shipping; unverified).
2. A competitor risk we did not have in the files: she says Indian brands already sell something similar for much cheaper. We need names, prices, ratings and what they actually do.
3. A cheap marketing opening: she plans to review cheaper Indian options. A fair review unit offer to creators like her is a low cost test (disclosure rules apply, check with the CLO and ASCI guidance, no paying for positive opinion, never fake).
4. The angle "give it to someone to hide for you" and "no scroll Sundays" are strong ritual angles that fit our "leave it in another room" message. The CMO can test them.
5. Platform gap: the screens in the reel look like iPhone. Her audience and the persona research both suggest many urban buyers are on iPhone, while nope is Android first. This is a real reach risk for Meta ads; flag to the CEO and CSO for the iPhone timing decision (do not change the plan without data).
6. Trust matters: the reel works because it is a real person with a real product, honest about uncertainty. Our version needs a real tap demo on camera, not a render.
