## 3. Wedge evaluation

(a) Personal-targets label checker. Matches the product plan and TruthIn already offers personalised flags (Plus tier), so the idea is not unique. Differentiation is only in rule transparency (user writes numeric targets, app reports pass or fail as fact). Easy to build, easy to copy. Weak alone.

(b) Protein value finder (protein per rupee, per 100 kcal, claim versus fact). No dedicated tool found. It plays to the audience's measurable obsession, is a plain fact calculation that stays inside the no-health-claims rule, and is shareable (rankings and screenshots travel on Reddit and Reels). The mislabelling study and the FSSAI tolerance debate give a content hook. Two constraints: price data. OFF has no reliable INR prices, so prices must be user-entered, or scraped from retailers (terms and brittleness risk). And "claim versus fact" can only compare front-of-pack claim to the label's own nutrition table, never to lab truth, unless the app imports lab results.

(c) Compare within category. Natural product shape and strong for the product, but it is a feature of (b) and of any scoring app. TruthIn can ship it.

(d) Better find: make (a) the engine and (b) the headline. "Protein value finder" is the entry product (public ranked tables by category: bars, whey, high-protein snacks, curd, paneer, oats), with personal targets and one-tap "meets my targets" as the retained-user layer. Claim-versus-label check = flag only when a front-of-pack claim contradicts the same pack's table, plus a "label tolerance" explainer sourced from FSSAI text once verified. (FSSAI 22.5 to 27.5 g band for a 25 g claim was reported by a health writer; I could not confirm it from an FSSAI document, https://tbthealth.substack.com/p/the-smart-consumers-guide-to-understanding. Verify before publishing.)

Pick: (d) as framed, lead with (b).

Defensible? Honestly, only modestly. Technology is copyable; TruthIn has the user base and could add protein per rupee. What can accrue: (1) a freshly maintained INR price plus macro dataset for roughly 300 to 500 protein SKUs (hard for a generalist app to keep current), (2) the recognised brand of "the protein maths people" on fitness social channels, (3) trust from facts-only presentation. This is a moat of niche attention and data upkeep, not a hard barrier. Expect a small, loyal audience, not a mass market.

Price anchors: Fittr coaching from Rs 175 (above), Apple Fitness+ India Rs 149/month or Rs 999/year (report 1). Test Rs 99 to 199 per year for saved targets, history, export (estimate). HealthifyMe conversion 1 to 2% (report 1).
3. B2B. Options (estimates, no benchmarks found): sponsored data fixes from brands are conflicted; safer are paid dataset or API access for gyms, coaches, and dietitians who want client-target sheets. Pink Tiger shows brands will pay for stamps, but that is a pay-for-verification model that undermines a facts-only stance. Avoid.
4. Ads: not attractive; low trust fit.

## 5. Zero-budget go-to-market and 90-day plan

Channels (tactics, no traction figures found): r/IndianFitness, r/bodybuilding India threads, r/FitnessIndia-type subs, Instagram Reels, YouTube fitness creators, gym WhatsApp groups, college gym clubs, Telegram.

- Days 1-30: Build a database of the 150 most-bought protein SKUs (whey, bars, snacks, curd/paneer, peanut butter) with label macros, pack size, price date stamped. Publish a free web page with sortable protein per Rs 100 and protein per 100 kcal, methodology stated, a "report a wrong value" button. Post the first table to Reddit with the method, not a link drop. 12 short reels ("protein per rupee: bars ranked").
- Days 31-60: Add personal targets (user sets numbers; app shows meets or not as facts). DM 10 small fitness creators (under 50k) with early access and a pre-built shareable card per product. Add HealthKart and Amazon affiliate links with disclosure. Weekly "price drop and rank change" post.
- Days 61-90: Launch claim-versus-label checks on a published set (front-of-pack claim against the table on the same pack; no lab claims). Test Rs 99 to 199 per year Pro. Pitch 3 to 5 coaches for a coach sheet. Targets (estimate, unsourced): 3,000 weekly users, 20 to 40 paying, first affiliate payout.
- Kill criteria (estimate): if by day 60 under 500 weekly users and no creator reshares, narrow to one category (whey) and go deeper.

## 6. Legal and reputational risks

- FSSAI "health drink": FSSAI said in March 2024 that "Health Drink" is not defined under the FSS Act 2006 and told e-commerce sites to categorise correctly; the Commerce Ministry advised removal of drinks including Bournvita from "health drinks" in April 2024. This is a listing-category instruction, not a general ban on the phrase. https://www.nutraingredients.com/Article/2024/05/14/fssai-clamps-down-on-e-commerce-sites-selling-health-drinks/ , https://www.businesstoday.in/latest/in-focus/story/bournvita-not-a-health-drink-govt-to-e-commerce-firms-425390-2024-04-13 . Action: the app should never use "health drink", "healthy", "clean" or "best for you" as a category label or caption. FSSAI Health Claims Regulations 2018 text: not retrieved in my searches; legal review needed.
- ASCI: April 2025 update to Addendum 2 distinguishes generic promotion from technical advice; credentials needed when giving technical advice, proof on request; advertisers responsible for influencers they hire; aligned with CCPA 2022 misleading-ads guidelines. Secondary coverage only. https://www.storyboard18.com/amp/advertising/asci-modifies-influencer-guidelines-update-for-health-and-finfluencers-63523.htm . Action: affiliate content must be disclosed, and creators we work with should not make health claims about products in our name.
- FoodPharmer suits: Mondelez v. FoodPharmer (Delhi HC allowed factual comment) and Madras HC permanent injunction in Triguni/EzeEats; see report 1 for URLs.
- Whole Truth precedent: even a clean-label brand drew ASCI scrutiny over "cleanest" wording (https://india.entrepreneur.com/?p=96355). Superlatives are the risk; numbers are not.
- Data accuracy risk specific to this segment: wrong macro or price data attributed to a named brand can be framed as product disparagement. Mitigate with source, date, user-reported-wrong button, and fast correction.
- How the user-set-targets, facts-only design changes risk: the app states "protein 18 g per 100 g; your target is 20 g or more; target not met" rather than any verdict on the product. Brand-defamation exposure falls because every statement is a verifiable label fact plus the user's own rule. Residual risks: (1) a locally calculated Nutri-Score shown as a grade can read as a health verdict, so keep it labelled informational and secondary (and swap to the official rating if FSSAI notifies one, report 1); (2) default target presets ("cutting", "bulking", "diabetic-friendly") imply health advice, so ship numeric presets only and name them by number; (3) affiliate ordering must not be influenced by commission; (4) claim-versus-label flags must say "front claim and table differ" not "false claim".
- No FSSAI or court action against a label-reading app found in my searches (not a clean legal opinion).

## Recommendation

Wedge: protein value finder (protein per rupee and per 100 kcal, category rankings, claim-versus-table checks) with personal targets as the retention layer. Path: free, then HealthKart and Amazon affiliate with disclosure, then Rs 99 to 199 per year Pro, then coach and gym dataset access. Biggest risk: TruthIn or a funded brand copying the protein-value view, and secondarily accurate, fresh INR price data at zero budget.
