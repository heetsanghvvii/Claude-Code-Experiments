## 2. Judging rubric for first messages and follow-ups

Each message is first checked against hard gates. Any gate failure caps the score at 39 regardless of other criteria. Passing messages are then scored on weighted criteria, each rated 0 to 4, and converted to a 100-point scale (criterion score ÷ 4 × weight).

**Hard gates (both message types):** fabricated or unverifiable personalization; wrong name, company or unfilled merge field; honorifics ("Sir", "Ma'am", "Respected"); over the channel's length limit; attachment, calendar link or image in a first touch; any implied guarantee of a job or referral; cold WhatsApp or SMS without permission; personal-life or appearance references; three or more questions.

### First-message rubric

| Criterion | Weight | 4 (excellent) | 2 (adequate) | 0 (fail) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Specific, genuine relevance (hook) | 20 | Opens with an uncommon shared tie or a named point from recent professional work; clearly unique to this recipient | Relevant but common hook ("fellow MBA", "love your company") | Generic or about the sender; passes the 50-recipient template test |
| Ask quality | 20 | One small, specific, expert-matched ask; answerable in 1 to 2 lines or a yes/no interest CTA; no job mention (or a justified, well-packaged posted-role exception) | One ask but vague ("any advice") or a proposed time slot | Referral or job ask to a stranger with no package, or no ask at all |
| Brevity and format | 15 | Email 50 to 100 words or DM under 400 characters; plain text; one paragraph or two short ones | Slightly long (100 to 120 words, 400 to 600 characters) | Over 120 words or 800 characters; bullets, résumé paste |
| Human voice (AI-suspicion test) | 15 | No banned tells; uneven sentences; one detail only this sender would know | One or two minor tells | Em dashes, stock AI vocabulary, tricolons, glossy polish |
| Recipient focus and credibility | 10 | Sender line is one relevant proof point, after the hook; message is about them | Sender intro first but short | Opens with "My name is", self-centred throughout |
| Ease of reply | 10 | Easy out; reply requires no research, no reading, no scheduling | No easy out but low effort | Requires reading a link or document, or demands time |
| Cultural and channel fit | 10 | Right greeting, register and channel for India and for the seniority gap; correct send window | Minor misfit (slightly too formal or casual) | Wrong channel, late-night timing, Hinglish to a stranger |

### Follow-up rubric

| Criterion | Weight | 4 (excellent) | 2 (adequate) | 0 (fail) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Adds new value | 25 | New observation, update, or narrower question that gives a fresh reason to reply | Restates the ask politely | Pure nudge with nothing new |
| Cadence compliance | 20 | 5 to 7 business days after first touch; first and only follow-up (second only for senior, no-network targets) | Slightly early (3 to 4 days) or a justified second | Same-day multichannel, a third or later follow-up, or under 3 days |
| Brevity and context | 20 | Under 60 words; references the earlier note in a few words | 60 to 100 words | Re-sends the whole first message or exceeds 100 words |
| No cliché or guilt | 15 | Free of "just checking in", "not sure if you saw", guilt or passive aggression | One mild stock phrase | Multiple clichés or guilt ("I haven't heard back...") |
| Appropriate escalation | 10 | Keeps the ask the same size or smaller after silence | Slightly larger ask | Escalates to a referral ask after no reply |
| Graceful close | 10 | Explicit easy out and a natural end to the thread | Neutral close | Demanding close or pressure to respond |

**Bands:** 85 to 100 send as is; 70 to 84 minor edits; 40 to 69 rewrite; under 40 or any gate failure, do not send.
